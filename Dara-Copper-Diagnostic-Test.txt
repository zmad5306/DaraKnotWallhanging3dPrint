COPPER SURFACE COMPARISON

Open Dara-Copper-Diagnostic-Test.3mf in Bambu Studio as a project.
One sliced plate for the Bambu A1 / 0.4 mm nozzle / textured PEI.
Use the copper SUNLU Silk 2.0 filament assigned to project filament 3.
Five identical 50 x 25 x 4.2 mm coupons; approximately 24.3 g and
1 hour 31 minutes, plus the time spent paused. Use normal 100% printer speed.

Viewed from above, with the FRONT of the printer nearest you:

                         REAR
                     [ S: solid ]

          [ C: 30 / 100 ]       [ D: 50 / 100 ]

          [ A: 30 / 104 ]       [ B: 50 / 104 ]
                         FRONT

Numbers are final top surface speed in mm/s / top flow multiplier in %.
A is the production control from when this comparison was built (104%).
Coupon C was subsequently selected for production. A-D use 15% gyroid; S uses 100%
rectilinear infill and the same 30 mm/s / 104% top settings as A.
Labels are in Bambu's object names, not embossed into the test surfaces.
Keep the pieces in position until you have marked their undersides.

All coupons retain the selected copper base filament flow ratio of 0.96,
220 C nozzle, 55 C bed, 0.2 mm layers, eight top shell layers / 1.6 mm,
50 mm/s internal solid infill, original wall layout, and no ironing.
The 100% and 104% values change only the TOP multiplier, not base flow.

INSPECTION

The project has one native pause before layer 21 (the final layer).
At that point all five coupons have completed Z=4.0 mm.
Photograph their surfaces under angled light, noting which already show
ridges or crosshatching. Leave them attached and resume promptly.
The final layer finishes at Z=4.2 mm. Once finished and cool, compare
surface feel, corner coverage, and gloss. Keep lighting consistent.

WHAT EACH COMPARISON TESTS

If ridges are already present before the final layer:
Compare A with S at the pause. A rough / S smoother means the fully solid
interior helped the underlying surface in this coupon. It supports further
work on roof support/construction; it does not prove a unique cause.
If both are rough, this test does not justify simply increasing infill.
The next separate test would be base filament flow-rate calibration.

If the next-to-last surface is smooth and the final layer adds ridges:
Compare A with B for speed at 104% flow, and C with D for speed at 100%.
Compare A with C for flow at 30 mm/s, and B with D for flow at 50 mm/s.
These comparisons show whether either tested change helps, including
whether it improves ridges at the cost of the corner coverage or gloss.
They are candidate adjustments, not guaranteed fixes.

The pause cools the surface, so keep it brief. A small flat coupon may not
reproduce the full part. Confirm a preferred result on the real geometry
before changing production. Creating this comparison did not change production;
the user's later selection of C is recorded in copper-finish-settings.json.
